### ★ l50_summary

![l50_summary](l50_summary.png)

L50, the fraction of links cut before half the living groups are lost, under both rules. Cutting the highest edge betweenness links first does more damage than random order (0.38 against 0.72), while cutting the weakest links first is the least damaging at 0.92.

### ★ link_robustness_all_prey_rule_strength

![link_robustness_all_prey_rule_strength](link_robustness_all_prey_rule_strength.png)

The link version of the targeted-versus-random comparison, cutting feeding links one at a time instead of removing whole groups. Cutting the strongest links first collapses the web after 57% of links are gone, random order takes 72%, and cutting the weakest links first takes 92% and produces no extinctions at all until roughly 82% of the links have been cut. So which links are cut matters a lot more than how many, and that is the clearest argument we have for treating the network as weighted rather than binary. A binary analysis would have no way of telling these three curves apart.
