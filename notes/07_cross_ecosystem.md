# Cross-ecosystem comparison

> Generated by [`code/compare_webs.py`](../code/compare_webs.py) from [`output/compare_webs_*.csv`](../output/). Rerunning the analysis rewrites this note, so its numbers always match the outputs.

**Facts for understanding, not text for the paper.**

## Why

Landry, on the methods draft (2026-11-08): *"apply your methods at least one other ecosystem to compare and contrast"*; on the proposal: *"if you look at other marine food web datasets, do your conclusions hold, and if not, is there a plausible explanation?"*

| FW_016_01 | Mondego estuary | Baeta, A. , Niquil, A., Marques, J. C., Patrício, J. | 36 | 240 | Ecopath diet composition |

All five record the same quantity — the share of each predator's diet made up of each prey — so the Cadiz methods apply unchanged. Each is cleaned the same way: the Ecopath boundary row (`Import`) dropped, detritus kept, cannibalism and weak links kept, every diet rescaled to sum to 1.

Two data quirks, both handled in the code:

- **FW_007 is recorded in percent**, not fractions. The matrix is divided by 100.
- **FW_007 also has 9 consumers whose listed diet does not add up** (Fish-eating birds 0.07, Catfish and stingrays 0.10, Gulls 0.14, Raptors 0.31, Red drum 0.47, Fish and crustacean-eating birds 0.51, Herbivorous ducks 0.89, Blue crabs 0.90, Deposit-feeding polychaetes 0.95). Rescaling those to 1 is a larger assumption than anywhere else, so St Marks' weighted results carry more uncertainty than the others.
- **FW_008** (Caribbean, Bascompte et al. 2005, 249 species) was left out: its diet columns sum to between 0.05 and 1.83, so its weights are not comparable.

## Verification

- The Gulf of Cadiz web is rebuilt here from the Web of Life API edge list, not from the project's own cleaned files, and is checked against the published outputs before anything is compared: the 43×43 culled matrix, all 43 node traits, 11 global measures, 14 R50 values and 14 correlations all agree exactly.
- One known discrepancy, found by that check: `network_structure.py` reads out-strength back from `cadiz_node_traits.csv`, where it is rounded to 4 decimal places. The ties that creates move ρ for importance-as-prey from −0.675 to −0.666 (`cadiz_centrality_vs_tl.csv` holds the rounded value). Immaterial, but the comparison uses the unrounded value.
- Two consecutive runs produce byte-identical CSVs, text and figure PDFs.
- Random orders: 1000 for node removal, 500 for link removal, seed 4200.
